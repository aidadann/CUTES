The **Rite of Christian Initiation of Adults** is how a grown-up becomes a
Catholic. It is not a course you pass. It is a journey the parish walks with
you, and it takes about a year because that is how long it takes.

## Who it is for

- Anyone who has never been baptised and wishes to be.
- Anyone baptised in another Christian church who wishes to be received into
  full communion with the Catholic Church.
- Catholics who were baptised as infants but never confirmed or never received
  First Communion.

## How the year runs

Sessions begin after the Feast of the Assumption and run weekly, usually on a
weekday evening in the parish hall. We read scripture, learn what the Church
believes and why, and pray together. There is no examination.

Along the way the parish celebrates the rites that mark your progress publicly
— the Rite of Acceptance, the Scrutinies during Lent — because this is the
parish's journey too, not only yours.

At the **Easter Vigil** the candidates are baptised, confirmed and receive the
Eucharist for the first time. It is the most important night of the year and the
whole parish is there.

## If you are not sure

Come anyway. Enquiring is not committing, and people leave the process without
being baptised every year without any hard feelings. The first step is a
conversation, nothing more.
