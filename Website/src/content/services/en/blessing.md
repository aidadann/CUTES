A blessing asks God's protection over a place, a thing, or a moment. The parish
is glad to give one, and there is no charge.

## House blessings

Most commonly asked for after moving into a new home, and traditionally during
the Easter season and around Chinese New Year. The priest or the deacon comes,
the family gathers, scripture is read, and every room is blessed with holy
water.

Prepare a small table with a crucifix, a lit candle and a bowl of water. Invite
the neighbours — a house blessing is a good excuse.

## Vehicles

A new car, a motorcycle, or a van used for parish work can be blessed after any
Mass. Ask the priest in the sacristy beforehand.

## Religious objects

Rosaries, medals, statues and scapulars are blessed after Mass on request.
A blessed object may not be sold.

## Other occasions

Examinations, a new business, a pregnancy, a journey, the start of an academic
year — the Church has a blessing for nearly everything. If you are not sure
whether to ask, ask.

## Blessing of the sick

Anointing of the Sick is a sacrament, not a blessing, and it is not only for the
dying. If you are facing surgery or living with a serious illness, ask the
parish priest to anoint you. It may be received more than once.
