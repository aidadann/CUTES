Marriage in the Church is not a ceremony the parish provides. It is a sacrament
the couple confers on each other, in front of the Church, and the preparation
takes it seriously.

## Start six months out

Speak to the parish priest **before** booking a hall, a photographer, or
anything else. Dates in the church are confirmed only after the paperwork is
under way, and couples who book the reception first sometimes discover the
preparation cannot be finished in time.

## The preparation course

The Archdiocese runs a Marriage Preparation Course over a weekend or several
evenings. Both parties attend, including a non-Catholic party. The certificate
is required before the wedding can be scheduled.

## Mixed marriages

A Catholic may marry a baptised non-Catholic Christian, or a non-baptised
person, with permission from the Archdiocese. The parish priest arranges this.
It is routine — it simply takes time, which is why we ask for six months.

## Convalidation

If you were married civilly and now wish to have the marriage recognised by the
Church, speak to the parish priest. The process is usually simpler than people
fear.
