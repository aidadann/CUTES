At the end of May and the start of June, Sarawak keeps Gawai Dayak and Sabah
keeps Pesta Kaamatan. Most of our East Malaysian students are still in Tanjung
Malim for it, sitting exams or finishing the semester, a flight away from home.

So CUTES keeps it here.

## The day

A Mass of thanksgiving for the harvest opens the celebration, with readings and
hymns in the languages the community actually speaks. Afterwards the parish hall
becomes something closer to a longhouse: traditional dress, *ngajat* and
*sumazau*, food that took three days to prepare, and the games.

## Everyone is invited

You do not need to be from Sabah or Sarawak, and you do not need to be Catholic.
Peninsular students, parishioners and families come every year, which is rather
the point — the festival travels with the people who keep it.

## Helping

Cooking starts two days before. The recreation ministry keeps the list.
